MODULE 2 · THE 3 PATTERNS OF AVOIDANCE
Pattern 1 of 3

The relationship cost feels more certain than the benefit.

Sarah
Fear of Friction

Daniel's deliverables have been slipping for weeks. Sarah needs to talk to him about it, but they've been friends for three years. She imagines the conversation and can already feel the room shift.

The cost feels certain. The benefit doesn't. So she says nothing.

What Sarah doesn't see Daniel's side of the story Reveal
What Sarah doesn't see

Daniel knows his work has slipped. He's been waiting for Sarah to bring it up, and she never does. To him, that silence means she's given up on him.

Silence doesn't protect the relationship. It hides the damage until it's too late.

How much does this sound like you?

Pattern 2 of 3

The skills that got you promoted are working against you.

Marcus
Autonomy Projection

Jin has been making repeated errors that affect the team's output. Marcus built his career by figuring things out alone, so giving Jin space feels like respect.

It doesn't occur to Marcus that Jin reads that space as indifference.

What Marcus doesn't see Jin's side of the story Reveal
What Marcus doesn't see

Marcus is now measured on what his team figures out, not what he solves alone.

Jin reads the silence as: Marcus hasn't noticed, or doesn't care.

How much does this sound like you?

Pattern 3 of 3

The system has taught you that avoidance is safer.

Amara
Political Defense

A team member's underperformance is affecting the whole group. But Amara's organization has shown her, through example not policy, that the person who raises a problem becomes the problem.

Her avoidance isn't weakness. It's pattern recognition.

What Amara doesn't see

The signal is real, but so is the cost. Two teammates are quietly picking up the slack.

One is starting to wonder why they bother.
