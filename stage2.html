Pattern 3 of 3
Amara

The system has taught you that avoidance is safer.

Amara isn't avoiding the conversation because she's afraid of conflict or because she doesn't know how.

She's avoiding it because her organization has spent two years showing her — through example, not policy — that the person who surfaces a problem becomes associated with it.

In Amara's context, avoidance isn't weakness. It's pattern recognition.

One more thing. This one is about your brain.

When you imagine having a difficult performance conversation, your brain treats it the same way it treats physical danger.

Your heart rate goes up slightly. Your thinking becomes less flexible. Your instinct is to escape the situation.

That is not a character flaw. That is your brain working exactly as it was designed to work.

The goal is not to stop feeling nervous.

The goal is to have a tool reliable enough that you can act even when you do.

What do you think is the estimated cost — in lost productivity, management time, and downstream team impact — of every single difficult conversation that is not held?
